You left the last lesson with a checkout and an awkward pause: a blockchain settles a payment by putting it in a block, and blocks take minutes. Dash is a network built around refusing that pause. This lesson shows how it gets a payment down to about a second, and what else that machinery turns out to be good for.

## Dash is digital cash, on purpose

Dash started as a fork of Bitcoin's code. It kept the core idea — money that moves peer to peer with no bank in the middle — and optimized for one use in particular: actually paying for things. It is a **cryptocurrency**, issued and settled by the network itself, with no government behind it the way there is behind <Term id="fiat">fiat</Term> currency.

Amounts work like dollars and cents, just with more decimals: one DASH is **100,000,000 <Term id="duff">duffs</Term>**, and duffs are what the protocol actually counts.

Two more facts set the scene. Blocks arrive roughly every **2.6 minutes**, with new coins issued as the reward for producing one. And the network launched with no <Term id="premine">premine</Term> and no <Term id="ico">ICO</Term>: no founder's allocation set aside before launch, no presale.

Which brings back the checkout, and a problem 2.6 minutes does not solve. Faster blocks are still far too slow for a customer standing at a counter. If waiting for blocks were the only way to be sure, Dash would be a slightly quicker version of something that still does not work for payments.

## A second tier, with money on the line

Bitcoin's network is one tier: every node does roughly the same job, and any machine can join or vanish anonymously at any moment. Dash keeps that tier and adds a **second one**, made of servers called **masternodes**.

A masternode is a server whose operator has locked up **1,000 DASH** as collateral. In return it performs extra work for the network and is paid for doing so. The collateral is the mechanism that matters, because it makes the second tier two things the first tier cannot be:

- **Known.** The network has an up-to-date list of exactly which servers are masternodes, because each one is anchored to a collateral transaction visible on the blockchain. You can address a specific set of servers instead of shouting into an anonymous crowd.
- **Accountable.** An operator who misbehaves risks their own stake, so their word costs them something to give.

An anonymous crowd cannot really be asked a question: you have no way to know who answered, or how many of the answers came from one person running a hundred machines. A known, bonded set can be. That difference is what the rest of Dash is built on — the second tier is why the network can offer services beyond passing transactions along, from near-instant payments to funding its own development out of the block reward.

## Back to the checkout: about one second

Here is the payoff. On Dash, a payment **confirms in about a second**.

When a transaction is broadcast, the masternode tier examines it and commits to two things: that this transaction is the one that will be mined, and that the outputs it spends are now locked against being spent anywhere else. That is exactly the guarantee you were waiting several blocks to obtain — the assurance that no competing transaction can take this money — and it arrives in roughly the time a card terminal takes to beep.

Blocks keep arriving every 2.6 minutes, and the transaction still lands in one permanently. You have simply stopped needing to wait for that before you act.

<Callout type="info" title="Why this changes what you can build">
The gap between about a second and a few minutes is the gap between a checkout that works and a screen that says *please do not close this tab*. It is what separates money you can spend from money you can only hold.
</Callout>

## The payment cleared. Where does the order live?

Your customer has paid. Now your app has to remember the order: what was bought, where it ships, who left the review afterwards. None of that is money, and the payments chain has nowhere to put it. On most blockchains this is the point where you go back to renting a database — and the parts of your app that mattered end up on someone else's server after all.

Dash's answer is to run **two jobs on one network**:

- **Dash Core** is the money layer — the blockchain everything above describes. It settles payments and carries the currency.
- **Dash Platform** is the application-data layer. It stores and validates the structured data behind real applications, on the same masternode tier.

Platform has two pieces you will meet constantly in this course. <Term id="drive">Drive</Term> is the storage: it keeps application data and enforces the rules about what may change it. <Term id="dapi">DAPI</Term> is the way in: your app reads and writes through it, so you never run a node yourself.

The two jobs meet on the servers that do both. <Term id="evonode">Evonodes</Term> are masternodes with a larger **4,000 DASH** collateral that host Platform on top of Core's services; regular masternodes serve Core only. Same network, same collateral-backed security, one more job.

## What you accomplished

You closed the gap the last lesson opened. Waiting for blocks was never the point — it was a way to be sure nobody could take the money back — and Dash's second tier of collateralized, known, accountable servers supplies that assurance directly, in about a second. That is what turns a 2.6-minute chain into something you can put behind a checkout.

You also reached the edge of the money layer: the order record a payments chain has nowhere to keep. That is Dash Platform's job, and it is where the next lesson starts.
